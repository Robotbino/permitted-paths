Part 1 · compounding

The effect of small errors increases

p steps correct → p to the power n

A long sequence of model decisions is not reliable, although each decision is good.

If you remove decisions from the sequence, the full task becomes more reliable.

Teaching model. The calculation holds only if the steps are independent and each has the same rate. In a real agent one wrong fact can cause many wrong steps, and a verifier can correct a wrong step. Use the chart for the direction of the effect, not for a number.

PART 1

Ask the room to guess the 90 per cent, 20 step number before you show it. Almost everyone guesses too high. A wrong guess is useful: it makes the result easier to remember. Then point at the amber line: a twenty-step task where each step is right nine times out of ten finishes correctly about one time in eight.
